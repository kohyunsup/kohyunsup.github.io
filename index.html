About

I study international security, with a focus on alliance politics, deterrence, and conflict below the threshold of war. My dissertation asks how the members of an asymmetric alliance divide the work of managing a shared adversary, and argues that junior allies can influence adversary behavior independently of their patron. The empirical work centers on the United States, South Korea, and North Korea, and rests on an original monthly dataset I built covering 1991 to 2024.

Before entering academia, I spent thirteen years working in national security policy for the Republic of Korea, including an appointment at the Office of the President. That experience shapes how I approach research: I am interested in questions that matter to the people who have to act on the answers, and in building evidence solid enough to be useful to them.
