About

I study international security, with a focus on alliance politics, deterrence, and coercive bargaining. My dissertation examines how alliance commitments shape the decisions states make about the use of force, and draws on an original dataset I built covering the period from 1991 to 2024.

Before entering academia, I spent thirteen years working in national security policy for the Republic of Korea, including an appointment at the Office of the President. That experience shapes how I approach research: I am interested in questions that matter to the people who have to act on the answers, and in building evidence solid enough to be useful to them.

I teach Introduction to International Relations at Syracuse University.
